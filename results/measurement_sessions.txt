=======================================================================
MEASUREMENT SESSIONS -- and why absolute timings differ between them
=======================================================================

Every timing in this project was re-measured in a second session, on the
same machine, against the same (deterministically generated) data. The
result is recorded here rather than quietly overwriting the first set,
because the difference between them is itself worth reporting.

                       SESSION 1        SESSION 2        RATIO
                       (median of 3)    (median of 5)    S1 / S2
  ------------------   -------------    -------------    -------
  Q1  OLTP                    1.12 s           0.56 s      2.0x
  Q1  star                    1.52 s           0.79 s      1.9x
  Q2  OLTP                   22.56 s           9.71 s      2.3x
  Q2  star (agg table)        0.03 s           0.03 s      1.0x
  Q2b star (bridges only)     4.08 s           2.19 s      1.9x
  Q3  OLTP                    0.80 s           0.40 s      2.0x
  Q3  star                    0.21 s           0.10 s      2.1x
  Q4  OLTP                    2.05 s           1.00 s      2.1x
  Q4  star                    1.26 s           0.66 s      1.9x

Every figure moved by almost exactly the same factor (~2x). That uniformity
is the clue: this is the machine running at a different speed, not the
queries behaving differently.


WHAT DID *NOT* CHANGE
---------------------
The query plans are identical between sessions -- same operators, same
join order, same row counts at every node. Q2's plan in both sessions:

    Session 1:  -> Limit: 20 row(s)   (actual time=21502..21502 rows=20)
                  ... Sort            (actual time=19190..19379 rows=2.06e+6)

    Session 2:  -> Limit: 20 row(s)   (actual time=10599..10599 rows=20)
                  ... Sort            (actual time= 9491.. 9584 rows=2.06e+6)

2,060,000 intermediate rows, both times, to the row. Same for every other
query. The DATA is deterministic (hash-seeded generation), the PLANS are
deterministic, and the ROW COUNTS are deterministic. Only wall-clock is not.


WHAT CAUSED IT
--------------
Not the buffer pool. That was the first hypothesis -- the pool is 128 MB
against 454.6 MB of data, so only ~28% of it fits in memory and cache state
could plausibly dominate. It was tested directly: Q2 was timed hot, then
the cache was deliberately evicted by scanning unrelated large tables, then
Q2 was timed again. It ran FASTER cold (7.43 s) than hot (10.55 s), which
rules the hypothesis out rather than confirming it.

What remains is ordinary machine variance -- CPU contention from other
work on the host, and thermal/frequency state. Session 2 ran with a load
average around 1.3 on 12 cores. Q2 alone was observed spanning 7.4 s to
12.1 s within a single session, which is +/-25% noise on its own.


WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE REPORTED RESULTS
-----------------------------------------
1. ABSOLUTE TIMINGS ARE NOT REPRODUCIBLE to two decimal places, on this
   machine or any other. Treat every figure as +/-25% at least, and expect
   a different machine to shift all of them together by some factor.

2. RATIOS ARE THE STABLE RESULT, and they hold across both sessions:

                            SESSION 1     SESSION 2
     Q1  star vs OLTP        0.74x          0.71x     (slower, both times)
     Q2b star vs OLTP        5.5x           4.4x
     Q3  star vs OLTP        3.8x           4.0x
     Q4  star vs OLTP        1.63x          1.52x

   Q1 is slower in both sessions. Q3 is ~4x faster in both. Q4 is ~1.5x
   faster in both. The conclusions in reflection.md rest on these, not on
   any single wall-clock number.

3. ONE FIGURE IS GENUINELY UNSTABLE and should be read with care: the
   aggregate-table speedup for Q2, quoted as 752x in session 1, is 324x in
   session 2. Both are "true" measurements. The instability is arithmetic,
   not physical -- the numerator moved (22.56 -> 9.71) while the
   denominator sat at the measurement floor (0.03 s, close to the cost of
   opening a connection at all). Any ratio with a near-zero denominator is
   fragile by construction.

   The defensible claim is "Q2 goes from ~10-20 seconds to effectively
   instant", not a specific multiple.

4. THE STRUCTURAL EVIDENCE IS UNAFFECTED. The argument in this project
   rests on row counts -- 2,060,000 intermediate rows to return 20, and a
   6.9x revenue overstatement of $838.7M reported as $5,752.3M. Those are
   properties of the schema and the data, reproduce exactly every time, and
   do not depend on how fast the machine happened to be running.
